Input GainCalibrate the level entering the Profile

Input Gain sets the internal working level before every other block; it does not replace the hardware preamp.

Live singingStart near 0 dB after setting the interface gain, then test the quietest and loudest passages while leaving headroom for EQ and compression.

Watch forIt cannot repair a signal clipped before the app. Excess gain overloads later blocks and changes Gate behavior.

  • Gain: raise or lower the whole Profile input.
Background AttenuationReduce steady room noise between vocal phrases

A smooth downward expander lowers fans, air conditioning, and room tone during quieter moments without fully closing the mic.

Live singingKeep breaths and soft endings intact, then increase Maximum Reduction gradually; it is usually a gentle cleanup stage before the Gate.

Watch forIt cannot separate noise that occurs with the voice. A high Threshold or fast closing action makes weak notes pump or disappear.

  • Maximum Reduction: deepest quiet-section attenuation.
  • Threshold: level below which attenuation begins.
  • Open / Close: recovery and fade-down speeds.
Noise GateClose the mic between phrases

Separate Open and Close thresholds prevent the Gate from chattering around one boundary.

Live singingMeasure room noise, then test breathy starts, consonants, and long phrase endings. More Hold and Release is often safer than simply raising the thresholds.

Watch forA Gate does not remove keyboard noise during singing. Tight settings cut breaths and tails. Detector HPF changes detection only, not the audible vocal tone.

  • Open / Close Threshold: enter and leave the open state.
  • Attack, Hold, Release: opening, retained-open time, and closing shape.
  • Closed Reduction: attenuation while closed.
  • Detector HPF: ignore low-frequency vibration in detection.
CompressorControl the difference between quiet and powerful phrases

Above Threshold, Ratio reduces further level growth; Makeup restores only the output level you need.

Live singingAim for moderate gain reduction on normal peaks, preserve the consonant attack, and compare at matched loudness before adding Makeup.

Watch forVery fast Attack dulls articulation, short Release pumps, and too much Makeup raises noise. Continuous heavy reduction is not loudness normalization.

  • Threshold / Ratio: where and how strongly compression begins.
  • Attack / Release: response and recovery times.
  • Makeup: post-compression gain.
  • Knee: transition around Threshold.
Equalizer (EQ)Remove unwanted frequencies and place the vocal in the mix

Six bands provide Low Cut, Shelf, Bell, and High Cut shapes with draggable or numeric control.

Live singingRemove unusable sub-bass first, then make broad, small cuts for mud and a restrained presence lift so the vocal reads over accompaniment without simply becoming louder.

Watch forNarrow high-Q boosts exaggerate resonances and feedback. Fixed “male/female” frequency recipes cannot replace listening to the singer, mic, and room.

  • Type / Frequency: filter shape and target area.
  • Gain / Q: amount and bandwidth.
  • Band Bypass: compare one band without deleting it.
SaturationAdd harmonics, density, or deliberate edge

Warm Vocal, Rock Edge, and Metal Bite move from subtle harmonic density to more obvious nonlinear grit.

Live singingBegin with low Drive and Mix for ordinary vocals; increase gradually for rock or screamed sections, then level-match with Output Trim.

Watch forLouder is easily mistaken for better. Excess Drive and Color amplify sibilance and noise, blur consonants, and consume limiter headroom.

  • Type: choose the nonlinear curve.
  • Drive / Color: intensity and character.
  • Mix: processed-to-direct balance.
  • Output Trim: match bypass level.
Air EnhancerAdd presence, breath, and high-frequency sheen

Mid Air emphasizes vocal presence; High Air adds the upper sheen and breathiness associated with a brighter capture.

Live singingUse a little Mid Air for lyric intelligibility, then add High Air conservatively. Pairing it with De-esser is often more natural than a large treble boost.

Watch forIt also exposes hiss, room noise, and sibilance. Match Output Trim before deciding that the result is clearer.

  • Mid Air: presence and definition.
  • High Air: breath and sheen.
  • Output Trim: level match and headroom.
De-esserReduce harsh S and SH consonants

De-esser attenuates the selected high-frequency region only while sibilance becomes dominant.

Live singingUse lyrics that reliably trigger sibilance and stop when harshness falls but consonants remain clear; placement after Air or bright EQ can catch the brightness those blocks add.

Watch forA low Threshold or excessive Maximum Reduction produces a lisp or dull vocal. The correct frequency depends on singer and microphone.

  • Frequency / Threshold: detection region and sensitivity.
  • Maximum Reduction: attenuation limit.
  • Attack / Release: catch and recovery timing.
Voice ChangerShift pitch and formant for character effects

Independent Pitch and Formant controls can change the note while adjusting perceived vocal-tract size.

Live singingBegin with small semitone and Formant moves or blend with Mix; select the nearest vocal range to help stable analysis.

Watch forLarge shifts sound synthetic and may increase Profile latency. This is a creative effect, not an anonymity or identity-protection tool.

  • Pitch: semitone shift.
  • Formant: perceived vocal-tract size.
  • Mix: shifted-to-direct balance.
  • Vocal Range: analysis aid, not output pitch.
Harmony (experimental)Create a key-aware supporting voice

Harmony follows the song Key/mode and lead pitch to generate diatonic thirds or octaves, fading when tracking confidence is insufficient.

Live singingConfirm the Key, use a low Mix for choruses or held notes, darken the harmony with Tone, and place it behind the lead with Width and Humanize.

Watch forA wrong Key, slides, screams, or unstable notes can confuse tracking. This is experimental and should not overpower the lead.

  • Direction: third above/below or octave above/below.
  • Mix / Width: level and stereo spread.
  • Humanize / Tone: variation and brightness.
  • Tracking: pitch and Key confidence requirement.
DoublerBuild double-tracked width and thickness

Two slightly delayed and detuned layers imitate repeated takes rather than a distinct rhythmic echo.

Live singingUse lower Mix in verses and switch to more width in a chorus; short Delay, slight Detune, and moderate Width are usually the most natural.

Watch forLarge Delay or Mix blurs consonants. Extreme width may become thin in mono, so check both stereo and mono before streaming.

  • Mix / Delay / Detune: level, timing, and cents offset.
  • Width: stereo placement.
  • Humanize / Tone: variation and brightness.
DelayAdd slap, KTV, or rhythmic echoes

The direct voice stays in place while Time creates repetitions; short settings produce slap and longer settings create audible echoes.

Live singingChoose Time to suit the song, filter echoes darker and thinner than the lead, and keep Wet and Feedback conservative for clear lyrics.

Watch forHigh Feedback accumulates for a long time or approaches runaway; one millisecond setting will not land on the beat at every tempo.

  • Wet / Time: echo level and interval.
  • Feedback: number and persistence of repeats.
  • Width: left/right timing spread.
  • Low / High Cut: clean and darken repeats.
ReverbCreate rooms, plates, or long ambience

Dense reflections establish depth; Pre-delay separates the lead from the tail, while Size, Decay, and Damping define the space.

Live singingUse Pre-delay to retain articulation, then set Decay and Wet. Chat Profiles need a short, low ambience; airy styles can be longer while keeping the lead forward.

Watch forA reflective real room plus long Reverb quickly loses clarity. Excess Wet and Decay mask the next phrase and make Gate transitions obvious.

  • Wet / Pre-delay: amount and onset separation.
  • Size / Decay: space scale and tail length.
  • Damping: bright or dark tail.
ShimmerAdd an octave-up halo to the reverb tail

Shimmer pitch-shifts the tail upward and feeds it back; Ducking lowers the effect under active singing and lets it bloom in gaps.

Live singingStart with low Wet, controlled Feedback, darker Tone, and enough Ducking for intelligible lyrics. Store the Profile default, then use the global Switch for a chorus.

Watch forBright tails build quickly. Block Bypass, Profile default state, and the temporary song Switch are separate controls.

  • Wet / Pre-delay / Decay: level, onset, and tail.
  • Feedback / Tone: buildup and brightness.
  • Ducking: suppress the halo under the lead.
LimiterCatch occasional vocal peaks at the Profile output

The Profile Limiter and Output Final Limiter are stereo-linked, lookahead sample-peak limiters, not true-peak limiters. Lookahead anticipates stored sample peaks; it does not reconstruct inter-sample peaks and is not True Peak.

Live singingUse the Profile Limiter as occasional peak protection, not constant heavy compression. The separate Final Limiter protects the formal Mix; its default −1.0 dBFS ceiling leaves pre-encode margin.

Watch forA −1.0 dBFS sample-peak ceiling does not guarantee AAC, resampling, or reconstruction remains below −1.0 dBTP. Use a true-peak-capable downstream tool when dBTP compliance is required.

  • Input Gain / Ceiling: drive and stored-sample limit.
  • Release: recovery after a peak.
  • Lookahead: preview sample peaks; adds Profile latency but not true-peak detection.
